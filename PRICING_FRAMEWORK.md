# BaytMiftah Pricing Framework

## Purpose
This pricing framework is designed for a business that owns and operates the BaytMiftah platform. The goal is to price based on value, customer complexity, and operational scope — not a rigid one-size-fits-all public menu.

The model must remain:
- flexible
- customer-specific
- margin-aware
- easy to explain to sales
- defensible in negotiation
- structured enough to protect the business

---

## Commercial Principles

1. Price by value and operational scope, not by default market imitation.
2. Protect a minimum recurring revenue floor for every customer.
3. Charge for support, onboarding, and premium scope beyond the base platform.
4. Keep a clear separation between standard SaaS subscription and custom services.
5. Let enterprise deals be negotiated, but under a documented commercial framework.
6. Use pilot pricing as a limited deployment strategy, not a permanent discount model.

---

## Pricing Formula

Total Monthly Price =
Base Platform Fee
+ (Unit Count × Unit Rate)
+ (Active Users × User Rate)
+ Module Charges
+ Support Tier Fee
+ AI/Automation Usage
+ Onboarding / Implementation Fee

### Example logic
- Base platform fee covers the standard core platform access.
- Unit rate scales with portfolio size and operational complexity.
- User rate captures organizations with many staff using the platform.
- Module charges apply for premium modules or specialized workflows.
- Support tier is an upgrade option rather than a hidden cost.
- AI usage is variable and should be either capped or billed separately.
- Onboarding is one-time setup and migration work.

## Customer Segments

### Segment A: Small Portfolio
Profile:
- 1–50 units
- small property managers or owner-operators
- limited complexity
- moderate user count

Commercial approach:
- lower recurring platform fee
- lower support burden included
- simple implementation
- optional add-ons only if needed

Typical structure:
- base fee: low / lean
- unit rate: low
- user rate: low
- support: standard support included
- onboarding: simple setup fee

### Segment B: Growth Portfolio
Profile:
- 51–500 units
- property management companies or multi-property operations
- multiple users and departments
- recurring finance, maintenance, and leasing workflows

Commercial approach:
- hybrid pricing model
- platform fee + unit/user scaling + support tier
- more module flexibility
- higher onboarding and training demands

Typical structure:
- base fee: moderate
- unit rate: scaled
- user rate: moderate
- support: standard or premium support
- onboarding: moderate implementation fee

### Segment C: Enterprise Portfolio
Profile:
- 500+ units
- multi-branch or layered operations
- regulatory and compliance requirements
- multi-role access, SSO, deeper operational oversight

Commercial approach:
- custom priced package
- contract-based commercial arrangement
- premium support SLA
- onboarding and training included or quoted separately
- custom reporting and security requirements

Typical structure:
- custom monthly fee with contract terms
- custom module bundle
- premium support
- implementation and migration fee
- optional AI/automation expansion

## Minimum Floor and Margin Rules

### Minimum recurring floor
Every customer should have a minimum recurring commercial floor to protect viability.

Rules:
- Do not sell below the operating floor for a customer profile.
- Smaller customers may be lower-touch, but still should not be underpriced enough to create service losses.
- Entering a deal below floor should require a clear strategic exception.

### Margin guardrails
Recommended guidance:
- maintain a healthy gross margin range based on support burden
- ensure support and implementation costs are included in pricing logic
- do not price purely from competition without considering operational cost

Suggested commercial rule:
- standard SaaS customers: protect a strong recurring margin
- enterprise: custom pricing with a defined support and SLA model
- pilot customers: temporary discounted terms allowed, but only with explicit success measurement

---

## Support Model

### Included support
Standard support should be clearly defined in the base package.

Examples:
- email support
- standard response times
- normal business hours support
- limited onboarding help

### Premium support
Premium support is a separate commercial layer.

Examples:
- faster SLA response windows
- phone support
- dedicated customer success contact
- priority escalation
- custom training and higher-touch implementation

This prevents free support from destroying margins.

---

## Module / Add-On Model

BaytMiftah should not make every capability part of a vague all-in bundle.

Modules can be structured as:
- core platform
- finance and accounting
- leasing and occupancy
- maintenance and service desk
- AI assistant and automation
- reporting and dashboards
- premium integrations
- custom onboarding and training

Commercial approach:
- define modules as included or add-on
- separate optional advanced features from the standard product
- price premium modules according to complexity and usage

---

## AI and Automation Pricing

AI should be treated as a usage-driven or capped capability, not as a free feature.

Options:
1. Included monthly AI allowance
2. Usage-based billing beyond included limit
3. AI package for premium users or enterprises
4. AI included for enterprise but separately monitored and budgeted

Recommended approach:
- base AI allowance included for standard customers
- premium AI packages or usage overages charged separately
- enterprise AI usage negotiated under contract terms

---

## Onboarding / Implementation Pricing

Implementation should not be bundled invisibly into the monthly price unless the business chooses to do so.

Typical charges:
- data migration
- company configuration
- property and unit setup
- workflow mapping
- training and go-live support
- custom reports or configurations

Commercial approach:
- standard onboarding fee for simple accounts
- longer implementation plan for complex portfolios
- fixed implementation quote for enterprise customers

---

## Pilot Pricing Structure

Pilot pricing must be explicit and limited.

Pilot commercial principles:
- short-term discounted pricing
- clear success criteria
- defined time window
- clear conversion path to full commercial contract
- limited support and usage parameters

Suggested pilot structure:
- 30–90 day pilot period
- reduced monthly fee or capped fee
- limited user count or property size
- defined onboarding support
- conversion to standard pricing at pilot end

This is a business tool for proving value, not a permanent low-price model.

## Example Commercial Templates

### Template A: Small portfolio
- Base platform fee: low
- Unit add-on: scaled by unit count
- User add-on: low
- Support: standard included
- Modules: select only necessary modules
- Setup: one-time simple onboarding fee

### Template B: Growth portfolio
- Base platform fee: moderate
- Unit rate: standard scale
- User rate: moderate
- Support: standard or premium
- Modules: selected bundle
- Setup: onboarding + training fee

### Template C: Enterprise
- Custom recurring fee
- Custom SLA and support terms
- Premium modules and integrations
- Implementation and migration fee
- Dedicated onboarding and success support

---

## Sales Pricing Guidance

Sales should not quote random numbers.

They should quote using:
- customer segment
- estimated unit count
- number of active users
- selected modules
- support tier
- required onboarding
- AI/automation level
- pilot or full rollout

This makes commercial discipline easier and gives finance and leadership a clear pricing logic.

---

## Recommended Commercial Outcome

The business should aim for a system where:
- each customer is priced from a repeatable formula
- the sales team has a structured quote framework
- the business can explain the price confidently
- price is not random or improvised
- the company can protect margin and service quality

This is the strongest way to commercialize BaytMiftah without locking the business into rigid pricing assumptions.
